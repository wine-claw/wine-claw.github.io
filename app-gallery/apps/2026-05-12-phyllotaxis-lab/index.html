About Phyllotaxis

Phyllotaxis is the study of how leaves, seeds, and petals arrange themselves in spirals on plants.

Most plants use an angle of about 137.5° — the golden angle — derived from the golden ratio φ. This creates the Fibonacci spiral patterns you see in sunflowers, pinecones, and artichokes.

Try nudging the angle slightly away from 137.5° and watch the pattern break down. Then nudge it back and see the spirals re-emerge.

Why 137.5°?
It's 360° ÷ φ², where φ ≈ 1.618. This irrational angle ensures seeds are packed with maximum efficiency and no two seeds align radially.

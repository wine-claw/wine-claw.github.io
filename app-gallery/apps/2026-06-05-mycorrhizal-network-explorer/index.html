Research Facts

• Mother trees can recognize their kin and preferentially send them more carbon.

• Fungal hyphae are only 1-2 micrometres wide — 50× thinner than a human hair.

• A single tree can be connected to hundreds of others through one fungal network.

• When a tree is attacked by insects, it sends chemical warning signals to neighbours.

• Older trees increase carbon transfer to seedlings as they become more shaded.

• Some fungi can transport nutrients up to 20 metres between trees.

• Networks are more active in summer when photosynthesis is high.

• Removing mother trees causes nearby seedlings to lose 30-40% of their carbon supply.

• Different tree species often share the same fungal species, enabling cross-species transfer.

• The mycorrhizal network is ancient — fungi have partnered with plant roots for 450 million years.

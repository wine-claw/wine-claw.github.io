Why the curve?

A great circle is the shortest path between two points on a sphere — it slices straight through the Earth’s centre. Pilots and ships often follow it (or a series of short straight segments) because it saves fuel and time.

A rhumb line keeps a constant compass bearing, so it looks straight on a Mercator map, but it is usually longer. The difference is tiny for short trips and dramatic for long east-west crossings.
